# Sample sources

All recordings are public domain ([CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/)), so no attribution is required. Each file is one note, named by the MIDI note it sounds, in a numbered take folder: `<kind>/<take>/<midi>.mp3`. Each note sounded plays a random take. All were trimmed, faded out, converted to mono, level-matched and encoded as 96 kbps mp3.

- `bell/`: [Chromatic handbells 12 tones](https://freesound.org/people/radwoc/packs/20825/) by radwoc, from Freesound. Sounds 370002, 370000, 370003, 369997 and 370001. Each file holds three techniques: single tone, double and ringing bell. Take `1` is the single strike. Take `2` is the main strike of the double, cut 5 ms before it so the quieter grace hit before it is left out, level-matched to take `1` over its first second, with peaks limited to −1.5 dBFS. The ringing bell is not used, because its strikes overlap.
- `xylophone/`: [Glockenspiel](https://freesound.org/people/cabled_mess/packs/19827/) by cabled_mess, from Freesound, a small glockenspiel recorded as several takes per note. Its filenames are three octaves below the sounding pitch, so `d3` is `86.mp3`. Takes `1`–`3` per note: 86 is 348873, 348875, 348872; 90 is 348888, 348887, 348886; 93 is 348891, 348890, 348893; 96 is 348908, 348909, 348910; 98 is 348917, 348915, 348916; 101 is 348922, 348923, 348924. The takes of each note closest in loudness were kept. Each is padded to 3 s, faded out from 2 s, matched to −24.5 dB RMS over its first second, and limited to −1.5 dBFS peaks. All are within 3 cents of pitch, so none are retuned.
- `whistle/`: Ocarina, Small (sustain) from the [Versilian Community Sample Library](https://github.com/sgossner/VCSL) by Versilian Studios. It has one take per note. Its filenames are an octave below the sounding pitch, so `ocarina_D5_sustain0` is `86.mp3`. `ocarina_A#4` and `ocarina_C5` are left out: they are 7–13 dB quieter than the rest and as breathy as they are tonal, so the lower whistles play `86.mp3` pitched down. Each file is retuned to its exact pitch, as some recordings are up to 16 cents sharp.
